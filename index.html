You do not have a marketing problem. You have a hiring problem — and your website does not mention hiring anywhere.

You told our team you already have a marketing team keeping you busy, and that the thing you cannot solve is finding qualified workers. So we audited your site for that instead of for leads. There is no careers page, no vacancy, no application form and no mention of joining the team on any page. A good gardener who admires your work has nowhere to put their hand up.

Your navigation reads Home, About Us, Grass Cutting, Garden Maintenance, Hedge Trimming, Pressure Washing, Get A Quote. Every route on your website is built to sell a job. None is built to fill one.

That matters more for you than for most, because of who your best applicant is. The gardener you actually want is already working for somebody else. He is not on Indeed at nine in the morning. He is in a van. He hears your name, or sees your vehicle, or a customer mentions you — and then he looks you up. At that moment your site tells him you are not hiring, because it says nothing at all.

Why The Usual Advice Has Not Worked
You told our team you have tried plenty of ways to find people

Almost every route a small firm tries first — Indeed, Facebook groups, a card in a shop window, asking around — is fishing in the same pool: people who are currently out of work. In a trade where the good ones are rarely unemployed for long, that pool is mostly people other firms have already passed on.

Job boards reach people actively unemployed Your website reaches people already working, who like what they see Which one has the gardener you want?

The second group never applies to an advert. They apply to a business. And they only do it when the business makes it easy, low-risk and quick — usually from a phone, in the evening, without a CV to hand and without telling their current boss.

That is the gap. It is not that you have not tried. It is that the channel that reaches the person you want is the one channel you have not built.

What You Have Already Got, And It Is The Hard Part
Most firms trying to hire have none of this
  • 44 reviews with an Excellent rating. This is your single strongest recruiting asset and it is currently only being used to win customers. Tradesmen read reviews about employers too.
  • Over a decade trading. Stability is the first thing a man with a mortgage checks.
  • A real customer base across sixteen towns — Barry, Penarth, Cowbridge, Llantwit Major, Dinas Powys and the rest. That is steady, local, year-round work rather than feast and famine.
  • Analytics already installed (GA4 tag live on the site). Rare, and it means we can measure applications from day one.
  • A tap-to-call number that works on mobile.
  • Customers who name you personally — “Ben and his amazing team”, “the boys”. That is what a good employer looks like from outside.

You are a business people would want to work for. You just have not told anybody that.

What A Hiring Page Has To Answer
In the order a working gardener asks them, at nine at night, on his phone
What's the pay? not on your site What are the hours? not on your site Is it year-round or seasonal? not on your site Do I need my own transport? not on your site What kit do you provide? not on your site Who would I be working with? not on your site Will my boss find out? not on your site

Every one of those is a reason not to bother. A man in secure work will not gamble an evening on a phone call to find out the pay is worse than what he has. Publish the answers and you remove the risk that stops him.

And the last question is the one nobody thinks about. Somebody currently employed will not ring a number and give his name to a stranger. He will fill in a short form on his phone with his thumb. That is why the form matters more than the phone number here — it is the exact opposite of your customer side.

The Thing Worth Checking Before Anything Else
One number in your brief we would like you to confirm

Our team recorded that you are doing 44 jobs a week. Your website displays 44 reviews. That may be pure coincidence — but the numbers matching exactly is worth thirty seconds of your time to correct, because everything we would build depends on your real job volume.

We have not guessed which is right and we have not built any figure on it. Tell us the real weekly number on the call.

Fix First — This Week, No Spend
  • Put a “Join the team” link in your main navigation. Right now there is no route at all
  • Publish the pay, the hours and whether it is year-round. The three questions that decide whether anyone bothers
  • Put your 44 reviews on the hiring page too — a good employer is proved the same way a good gardener is
  • Photograph the team, the vans and the kit. Applicants are buying a working day, not a job title
Build Next
  • A short application form built for a thumb — experience, transport, tickets, availability. No CV required
  • Screening built into the form so a qualified gardener and a time-waster do not arrive looking identical
  • Applications tracked as conversions in your existing GA4, so you know which route produces people
  • Then advertise the vacancy locally — into a page that can actually take an application

Ten years, 44 reviews, sixteen towns — and no way for a good gardener to say “are you hiring?”

The reputation that wins you customers would win you staff too. It is pointed entirely at one of those and not at all at the other.
